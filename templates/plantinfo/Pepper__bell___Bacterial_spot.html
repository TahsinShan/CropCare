🫑 Pepper Bell - Bacterial Spot

📖 Overview

Bacterial spot in bell pepper, caused by Xanthomonas species (such as X. euvesicatoria, X. vesicatoria), is a common leaf, fruit, and stem disease in warm, humid regions. It leads to spotted leaves, fruit blemishes, defoliation, and can significantly lower marketable yield and quality. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}

🧬 Causes & Pathogen Biology

The pathogen survives on infected seed, in crop debris, volunteer pepper plants, and on weeds. It spreads via splashing water (rain, overhead irrigation), windblown moisture, and mechanical contact (handling, tools). Infection occurs through natural openings, wounds, or when leaf surfaces remain wet. :contentReference[oaicite:2]{index=2}

Optimal Spread Conditions:

  • Temperature: ~24–30 °C (75–86 °F) :contentReference[oaicite:3]{index=3}
  • Relative humidity high and frequent leaf wetness (due to rain, dew or overhead irrigation) :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}
  • Poor air circulation and dense foliage :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}
  • Use of infected seed or transplants :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}

🌡 Symptoms & Diagnostic Clues

  • Small, water-soaked or greasy spots on underside of leaves; later becoming brown to brown‑black :contentReference[oaicite:7]{index=7}
  • Leaf spots irregular or round; centers may fall out (“shot‑hole” appearance) creating a ragged look :contentReference[oaicite:8]{index=8}
  • Yellowing around lesions; defoliation in severe cases :contentReference[oaicite:9]{index=9}
  • Stem lesions: dark streaks or raised cankers :contentReference[oaicite:10]{index=10}
  • Fruit lesions often near stem end; start water‑soaked, then become raised, rough or scabby; may crack or become necrotic :contentReference[oaicite:11]{index=11}

📊 Disease Progression Stages

Stage 1: Early Infection

Initial small water‑soaked spots appear on lower leaves, mostly underside. Mild symptoms, no fruit or stem damage yet.

Stage 2: Moderate Infection

Spots enlarge and darken; some leaf tissue dies, yellow halos may form, early lesions on stems or petioles. Some fruit begin showing spots. Defoliation of older leaves begins.

Stage 3: Severe Infection

Widespread leaf necrosis, heavy defoliation, many raised or scabby spots on fruit, stem cankers, fruit cracking. Yield and fruit quality greatly reduced. Plants may look ragged.

🛠 Treatments & Control Options

  • Use disease‑free, pathogen‑tested seed and certified transplants. :contentReference[oaicite:12]{index=12}
  • Apply copper‐based bactericides when disease risk is high. :contentReference[oaicite:13]{index=13}
  • Use of resistance‑inducing agents (e.g. acibenzolar‑S‑methyl) in some cases. :contentReference[oaicite:14]{index=14}
  • Avoid overhead irrigation; use drip or furrow irrigation to reduce leaf wetness. :contentReference[oaicite:15]{index=15}
  • Sanitize tools, avoid working in wet foliage, remove infected debris. :contentReference[oaicite:16]{index=16}

🌿 Cultural Prevention Techniques

  • Rotate out of peppers (or other Solanaceae) for at least 1–2 years. :contentReference[oaicite:17]{index=17}
  • Use resistant or tolerant bell pepper varieties where available. :contentReference[oaicite:18]{index=18}
  • Ensure good spacing and pruning to improve air circulation. :contentReference[oaicite:19]{index=19}
  • Manage moisture: avoid overhead watering, ensure foliage dries quickly after rain. :contentReference[oaicite:20]{index=20}
  • Remove volunteer plants, weeds, and crop residues that can harbor bacteria. :contentReference[oaicite:21]{index=21}

📅 Early Detection & Monitoring

Scout plants regularly—especially lower leaves—for water‑soaked or greasy spots under humid, wet conditions. Monitor weather (humidity, rainfall). Once spots are noticed, track progression to act before heavy spread occurs. :contentReference[oaicite:22]{index=22}
