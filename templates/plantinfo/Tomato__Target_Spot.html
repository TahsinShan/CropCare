🍅 Tomato Target Spot

📖 Overview

Target spot, caused by the fungus Corynespora cassiicola, is a common foliar disease of tomatoes that occurs in warm and humid climates. It can cause significant leaf spot lesions, defoliation, and yield loss. The disease may also infect stems and fruit, causing blemishes and quality reduction.

🧬 Causes & Pathogen Biology

Corynespora cassiicola survives in plant debris and soil. It produces conidia (asexual spores) that are spread by wind and splashing water. Infection occurs when spores land on wet leaf surfaces and germinate, penetrating through stomata or wounds.

Optimal Spread Conditions:

  • Temperature: 24–30°C (75–86°F)
  • Relative Humidity: ≥ 90%
  • Leaf wetness duration: ≥ 8 hours
  • Frequent rainfall or overhead irrigation

🌡 Symptoms & Diagnostic Clues

  • Distinctive brown, circular lesions with concentric rings ("target" appearance)
  • Lesions initially appear on lower leaves and expand upward
  • Leaf yellowing and premature leaf drop
  • Lesions may also appear on stems and green fruit, causing small, sunken spots
  • Severe infections lead to extensive defoliation and reduced yield

📊 Disease Progression Stages

Stage 1: Early Infection

Small brown spots with faint concentric rings develop on lower leaves. No stem or fruit symptoms yet.

Stage 2: Moderate

Lesions enlarge, showing characteristic target-like rings. Yellowing and curling of leaves. Some lesions on stems may appear.

Stage 3: Severe

Extensive leaf necrosis and defoliation. Fruit spots visible causing cosmetic damage. Yield and fruit quality decline noticeably.

🛠 Treatments & Control Options

  • Apply fungicides such as chlorothalonil, mancozeb, or strobilurin-class fungicides
  • Begin treatments preventively or at first signs of symptoms
  • Rotate fungicides to reduce risk of resistance development
  • Remove and destroy infected plant debris to reduce inoculum
  • Improve air circulation and avoid overhead irrigation

🌿 Cultural Prevention Techniques

  • Practice crop rotation with non-host plants for 2–3 years
  • Space plants adequately to improve airflow and reduce humidity
  • Use drip irrigation instead of overhead watering
  • Sanitize tools and greenhouse surfaces regularly
  • Remove volunteer tomato plants and weeds that may harbor the pathogen

📅 Early Detection & Monitoring

Scout lower and mid canopy weekly during warm, humid conditions. Look for characteristic target-shaped lesions. Track weather data to anticipate periods of high risk and time fungicide applications accordingly.
