🍅 Tomato Late Blight

📖 Overview

Late blight, caused by the oomycete Phytophthora infestans, is a devastating disease of tomatoes and potatoes. It thrives under cool (15–20°C) and humid (>90% RH) conditions. Historically, it contributed to the Irish Potato Famine in the 1840s.

🧬 Causes & Pathogen Biology

P. infestans is a water mold, not a true fungus. It produces sporangia and zoospores that are dispersed by wind or water. These infect plant tissues through stomata or wounds, causing necrosis and systemic spread.

Optimal Spread Conditions:

  • Temperature: 12–20°C (54–68°F)
  • Relative Humidity: ≥ 85%
  • Leaf wetness duration > 10 hours

🌡 Symptoms & Diagnostic Clues

  • Dark brown, water-soaked lesions on lower leaves
  • Gray-white fuzzy mold under high humidity
  • Stem lesions may cause plant collapse
  • Brown to black firm lesions on green fruit

📊 Disease Progression Stages

Stage 1: Mild

Small irregular dark spots on older foliage. May be mistaken for nutrient deficiency or minor damage.

Stage 2: Moderate

Lesions enlarge with yellow halos. Under humid conditions, white mold appears on undersides of leaves.

Stage 3: Severe

Widespread necrosis, fruit damage begins. Some branches show dieback. Spores can be found with hand lens.

🛠 Treatments & Control Options

  • Remove infected tissues promptly to reduce inoculum.
  • Use copper-based fungicides or systemic options like chlorothalonil (if permitted).
  • Apply fungicides preventively during weather risk windows.
  • Maintain good leaf drying via drip irrigation and spacing.

🌿 Cultural Prevention Techniques

  • Practice 3–4 year crop rotation with non-host crops (e.g., corn, beans).
  • Use resistant cultivars like 'Mountain Magic', 'Defiant PhR'.
  • Clean tools with 10% bleach solution or alcohol.
  • Mulch to prevent rain splash and lower leaf wetness.

📅 Early Detection & Monitoring

Monitor leaves weekly using a hand lens for early sporulation. Use forecasting tools (e.g., BlightCast) to anticipate outbreaks.
