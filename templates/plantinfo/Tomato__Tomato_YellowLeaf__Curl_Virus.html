🍅 Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus

📖 Overview

Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV) is a devastating viral disease transmitted by the whitefly Bemisia tabaci. It causes severe stunting, leaf curling, yellowing, and reduced fruit yield and quality. TYLCV is a major threat in warm climates worldwide.

🧬 Causes & Pathogen Biology

Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV) is a geminivirus transmitted persistently by the whitefly Bemisia tabaci. The virus survives in infected plants and is spread when whiteflies feed on healthy plants, introducing the virus into the phloem.

Optimal Spread Conditions:

  • Temperature: 25–35°C (77–95°F)
  • High whitefly populations
  • Warm, dry conditions favor whitefly activity
  • Presence of infected host plants or weeds

🌡 Symptoms & Diagnostic Clues

  • Severe upward leaf curling and cupping
  • Leaf yellowing, especially along leaf margins and veins
  • Stunted growth and shortened internodes
  • Reduced flowering and fruit set
  • Small, malformed fruit with poor quality

📊 Disease Progression Stages

Stage 1: Early Infection

Whiteflies present on young leaves. Initial mild yellowing and slight leaf curling start to appear.

Stage 2: Moderate Infection

Leaf curling becomes more pronounced with extensive yellowing. Plant growth slows and internodes shorten.

Stage 3: Severe Infection

Severe stunting and leaf deformation. Flowering and fruit production drastically reduced. Fruit that develops is small and malformed.

🛠 Treatments & Control Options

  • Manage whitefly populations with insecticides such as neonicotinoids or insect growth regulators
  • Use reflective mulches to repel whiteflies
  • Remove and destroy infected plants to reduce virus sources
  • Implement insect-proof screens in greenhouses
  • Grow resistant or tolerant tomato varieties where available

🌿 Cultural Prevention Techniques

  • Practice crop rotation with non-host plants
  • Maintain good weed control to reduce alternate virus hosts
  • Use yellow sticky traps to monitor and reduce whitefly populations
  • Sanitize tools and greenhouse structures regularly
  • Space plants adequately to reduce whitefly spread

📅 Early Detection & Monitoring

Monitor whitefly populations weekly using yellow sticky traps and visual scouting. Inspect young plants for early yellowing and leaf curling. Time insecticide applications based on whitefly activity to prevent virus transmission.
