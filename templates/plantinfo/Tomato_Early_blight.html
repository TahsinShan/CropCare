🍅 Tomato Early Blight

📖 Overview

Early blight, primarily caused by Alternaria solani, is a common foliar disease affecting tomatoes and potatoes. It is favored by warm, humid conditions and often leads to premature defoliation, reducing fruit quality and yield.

🧬 Causes & Pathogen Biology

A. solani is a necrotrophic fungus that survives in plant debris and soil. It produces conidia (asexual spores) that are spread by wind, rain splash, and mechanical contact. Infection typically begins on older, lower leaves and progresses upward.

Optimal Spread Conditions:

  • Temperature: 24–29°C (75–85°F)
  • Relative Humidity: ≥ 90%
  • Leaf wetness > 8 hours promotes spore germination

🌡 Symptoms & Diagnostic Clues

  • Brown concentric ring lesions (“target spots”) on lower leaves
  • Yellowing and defoliation starting from the bottom upward
  • Stem lesions at pruning or injury sites
  • Dark, sunken lesions on fruits near the stem end

📊 Disease Progression Stages

Stage 1: Initial

Small dark spots on older leaves. Often unnoticed early on, especially in shaded lower canopy.

Stage 2: Moderate

Multiple leaves affected. Progressive defoliation from bottom to mid canopy. Early fruit sunscald risk increases.

Stage 3: Severe

Extensive defoliation, fruit lesions common. Reduced yield and marketability. Plant vigor severely compromised.

🛠 Treatments & Control Options

  • Apply fungicides like mancozeb, chlorothalonil, or copper-based products at early signs
  • Begin treatment early in the season and reapply every 7–10 days during wet periods
  • Remove infected leaves and avoid working in fields while foliage is wet
  • Ensure adequate plant spacing and airflow

🌿 Cultural Prevention Techniques

  • Use crop rotation with non-solanaceous crops for 2–3 years
  • Choose resistant cultivars such as 'Iron Lady', 'Mountain Fresh Plus'
  • Mulch soil to reduce splash dispersal
  • Irrigate at the base, avoid overhead watering
  • Sanitize tools and equipment between rows

📅 Early Detection & Monitoring

Conduct weekly inspections, especially after rain or high humidity events. Use field scouting forms and mobile apps like IPM Tools for tracking symptom spread.
