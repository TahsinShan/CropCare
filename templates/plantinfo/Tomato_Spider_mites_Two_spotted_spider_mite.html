🕷️ Tomato Two-Spotted Spider Mite

📖 Overview

The two-spotted spider mite (Tetranychus urticae) is a common pest affecting tomato plants, especially under hot, dry conditions. These tiny arachnids feed on leaf tissue, causing stippling, leaf bronzing, and in severe cases, leaf drop and reduced yield.

🧬 Causes & Pest Biology

Tetranychus urticae thrives in warm, dry environments. They reproduce rapidly, with populations building up quickly during hot weather. Mites feed by piercing leaf cells and sucking out contents, leading to damage.

Optimal Conditions for Spread:

  • Temperature: 27–32°C (80–90°F)
  • Low Relative Humidity: 40–60%
  • Dry, dusty conditions
  • High plant density and poor air circulation

🌡 Symptoms & Diagnostic Clues

  • Fine stippling or speckling on upper leaf surfaces
  • Bronzed or yellowed leaves with a dry, dusty appearance
  • Presence of fine webbing on leaves and stems in heavy infestations
  • Leaf curling and premature leaf drop in severe cases
  • Reduced plant vigor and lower fruit yield

📊 Pest Population & Damage Stages

Stage 1: Early Infestation

Small groups of mites on underside of lower leaves. Minor stippling visible. No webbing yet.

Stage 2: Moderate Infestation

Increased mite numbers, noticeable stippling and yellowing. Early webbing may appear on leaf undersides.

Stage 3: Severe Infestation

Heavy webbing covers leaves and stems. Extensive bronzing, leaf curling, and drop. Significant yield loss likely.

🛠 Treatments & Control Options

  • Apply miticides such as abamectin, spiromesifen, or bifenthrin
  • Use insecticidal soaps or horticultural oils for early infestations
  • Introduce natural predators like predatory mites (Phytoseiulus persimilis)
  • Rotate chemical classes to prevent resistance buildup
  • Maintain proper irrigation and avoid plant stress

🌿 Cultural Prevention Techniques

  • Keep plants well-watered to reduce stress and susceptibility
  • Increase air circulation by proper spacing and pruning
  • Regularly clean greenhouse and tools to remove mites and eggs
  • Remove weeds and alternate hosts that harbor spider mites
  • Monitor dust levels and reduce dust accumulation

📅 Early Detection & Monitoring

Inspect undersides of lower leaves weekly, especially during hot, dry periods. Use a hand lens to spot tiny mites and eggs. Record infestation levels and apply control measures promptly to prevent outbreaks.
