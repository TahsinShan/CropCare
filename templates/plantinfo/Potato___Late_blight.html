🥔 Potato Late Blight

📖 Overview

Late blight, caused by the oomycete Phytophthora infestans, is a devastating disease of potatoes that thrives in cool, wet conditions. It rapidly affects leaves, stems, and tubers, causing dark lesions, rot, and significant yield loss. It is infamous for causing the Irish potato famine.

🧬 Causes & Pathogen Biology

Phytophthora infestans survives in infected plant debris, volunteer potatoes, and tubers. It produces sporangia that are dispersed by wind and water. Infection occurs when spores land on wet surfaces and germinate, penetrating plant tissues through stomata or wounds.

Optimal Spread Conditions:

  • Temperature: 10–21°C (50–70°F)
  • Relative Humidity: ≥ 90%
  • Leaf wetness duration: ≥ 6 hours
  • Frequent rainfall or heavy dew

🌡 Symptoms & Diagnostic Clues

  • Dark brown to black, water-soaked lesions on leaves that rapidly enlarge
  • Lesions often have a fuzzy white or gray mold on the underside in humid conditions
  • Leaf yellowing and rapid defoliation
  • Dark, sunken lesions on stems and leaf petioles
  • Tuber infections causing firm, dark brown rot with irregular edges
  • Rapid disease progression can cause entire plants to collapse

📊 Disease Progression Stages

Stage 1: Initial Infection

Small, dark water-soaked spots appear on leaves and stems. No tuber symptoms yet.

Stage 2: Moderate

Lesions enlarge and coalesce, white mold visible on undersides of leaves during humid conditions. Some stem lesions and early tuber infections develop.

Stage 3: Severe

Extensive leaf necrosis and defoliation. Stem lesions widespread. Tubers show dark, soft rot. Plants may collapse completely, causing major yield loss.

🛠 Treatments & Control Options

  • Apply fungicides such as metalaxyl, chlorothalonil, or copper-based fungicides preventively or at first symptoms
  • Rotate fungicides and follow label recommendations to avoid resistance
  • Remove and destroy infected plant debris and volunteer potatoes
  • Use certified disease-free seed tubers
  • Improve air circulation and avoid overhead irrigation to reduce leaf wetness

🌿 Cultural Prevention Techniques

  • Practice crop rotation with non-host crops for 3–4 years
  • Use resistant potato varieties when available
  • Hill soil around plants to protect tubers from infection
  • Maintain balanced fertilization to support plant health
  • Regularly remove volunteer potatoes and weeds

📅 Early Detection & Monitoring

Scout fields regularly, especially during cool, wet weather. Look for water-soaked lesions and white mold on leaf undersides. Monitor weather conditions and use disease forecasting models to time fungicide applications.
