🍅 Tomato Bacterial Spot

📖 Overview

Bacterial spot, caused by Xanthomonas spp., is a common bacterial disease of tomatoes that leads to leaf spots, fruit lesions, and defoliation. It thrives in warm, wet conditions and can significantly reduce yield and fruit quality.

🧬 Causes & Pathogen Biology

Xanthomonas spp. bacteria survive on seeds, plant debris, and weeds. They spread through rain splash, irrigation water, and contaminated tools. Infection occurs when bacteria enter through natural openings or wounds on leaves, stems, and fruit.

Optimal Spread Conditions:

  • Temperature: 24–29°C (75–85°F)
  • High Relative Humidity and leaf wetness
  • Frequent rainfall or overhead irrigation
  • Wounds or natural openings on plant surfaces

🌡 Symptoms & Diagnostic Clues

  • Small, water-soaked spots on leaves that enlarge and turn brown to black
  • Spots often have yellow halos and may merge to form large necrotic areas
  • Lesions on stems appear as dark, sunken spots
  • Fruit shows raised, rough, dark lesions that can crack
  • Severe infections cause leaf drop and reduced fruit quality

📊 Disease Progression Stages

Stage 1: Initial Infection

Small water-soaked spots on lower leaves, often overlooked. No stem or fruit symptoms yet.

Stage 2: Moderate Infection

Spots enlarge with yellow halos on leaves. Stem lesions appear. Early fruit spots visible.

Stage 3: Severe Infection

Extensive leaf necrosis and defoliation. Fruit lesions crack and degrade quality. Yield loss significant.

🛠 Treatments & Control Options

  • Apply copper-based bactericides regularly during wet weather
  • Use resistant tomato varieties when available
  • Implement proper sanitation and crop rotation
  • Avoid overhead irrigation to reduce leaf wetness
  • Remove and destroy infected plant debris promptly

🌿 Cultural Prevention Techniques

  • Use certified disease-free seed or transplants
  • Practice crop rotation with non-host crops for 2–3 years
  • Space plants to improve air circulation and reduce humidity
  • Sanitize tools and equipment regularly
  • Remove volunteer plants and weeds that can harbor bacteria

📅 Early Detection & Monitoring

Regularly scout plants during warm, wet periods for small water-soaked spots on leaves and fruit. Monitor weather conditions to anticipate outbreaks and time bactericide applications effectively.
