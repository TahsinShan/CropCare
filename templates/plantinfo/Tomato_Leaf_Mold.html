🍅 Tomato Leaf Mold

📖 Overview

Leaf mold is a foliar fungal disease of tomatoes caused by Passalora fulva (formerly Fulvia fulva). It primarily affects greenhouse and high-tunnel grown tomatoes but can also occur in open fields under warm, humid conditions. While it rarely infects fruit, severe cases can reduce photosynthetic area, impacting yield.

🧬 Causes & Pathogen Biology

Passalora fulva is an airborne fungal pathogen that survives on infected plant debris and greenhouse surfaces. It produces conidia (asexual spores) that germinate on wet leaves and penetrate via stomata.

Optimal Spread Conditions:

  • Temperature: 20–25°C (68–77°F)
  • Relative Humidity: ≥ 85%
  • Leaf wetness duration > 9 hours
  • Poor ventilation in greenhouses favors rapid spread

🌡 Symptoms & Diagnostic Clues

  • Yellow spots on upper leaf surfaces
  • Velvety olive-green to gray mold on undersides of leaves
  • Infected leaves curl, dry out, and fall off
  • Rarely affects stems or fruit directly

📊 Disease Progression Stages

Stage 1: Early

Small, pale-yellow spots form on the upper leaf surface. No symptoms on stems or fruit. High humidity initiates infection.

Stage 2: Moderate

Olive-green velvety mold appears on undersides of leaves. Spots enlarge, leaves start curling. Lower canopy most affected.

Stage 3: Severe

Significant leaf drop. Photosynthesis declines. Fruit exposed to sunscald and development slows. High spore loads in air.

🛠 Treatments & Control Options

  • Use fungicides such as chlorothalonil, mancozeb, or strobilurin-class (QoI) fungicides (e.g., azoxystrobin)
  • Start applications preventively or at first sign of symptoms
  • Rotate fungicides to prevent resistance
  • Prune lower foliage and increase air movement in greenhouses

🌿 Cultural Prevention Techniques

  • Improve ventilation in protected environments
  • Avoid overhead irrigation; water early in the day if needed
  • Space plants adequately to reduce humidity buildup
  • Sanitize greenhouses, tools, and trays between crops
  • Use resistant cultivars where available (e.g., ‘Trust’, ‘Valoria’)

📅 Early Detection & Monitoring

Inspect lower canopy weekly in humid periods. Use yellow sticky traps to monitor spore movement. Maintain environmental logs to track humidity and temperature spikes.
